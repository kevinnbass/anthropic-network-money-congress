# The 29 figures: what each shows, and what cuts against it

Each entry below is the project's own verdict for the figure, word for word as its working README records it, with the limits that cut against the reading. Names like X33 or C130 are the project's labels for its audits and collection batches; `research/...` paths are files in this repository or the project's working files. Every figure comes as `.html` (the page itself, with the ids of the rows it draws in a hidden comment), `.png` and `.jpg`.

## 01. Which of the twelve kinds of connection reach which members

[`congress-01-connection-grid.jpg`](figures/congress-01-connection-grid.jpg) · [`.png`](figures/congress-01-connection-grid.png) · [`.html`](figures/congress-01-connection-grid.html)

**What it shows.** 435 of the 535 sitting members have at least one verified row in some connection type; 100 have none (five of the 100 sponsored an AI bill but have no connection row). The types that reach the most members are 4b lobbyists' own contributions made while their firm was registered for a network client (293 members, $1.2M, hop 2), 1 individual hard money (202 members, $5.2M, gifts to a member's single-member joint fundraising committee or leadership PAC included after audit X45), 3b independent expenditures by committees into which the record holds an itemised network receipt (201, hop 2), 2 PAC money from ARI PAC, ANTHROPAC or a registrant's PAC (118; $46,500 of the $328K is the Arnold & Porter partners' PAC, the only PAC money 20 of the 118 received), 11 public statements naming the network (63), 9 travel and events (41) and 5 personnel ties in either direction (39 members: 11 with a network-to-Hill row and 29 with a Hill-to-network row, one with both; fellowship placements come from Horizon's roster after lane C130, and TechCongress placements are context). Type 10 reaches 21 members for about $20K; type 8 reaches two members through Carnegie Endowment household income; type 7 reaches none (the one proposed amendment naming RAND is outside RAND's network-funded lines, audit X33b); types 3a and 6 reach none directly (receipts and grants go to committees and organisations, not members).

**What cuts against it.** The two widest types are second hop. Type 3b's dollar total ($526M across 201 members, after a census of its 9,855 Schedule E lines and one Form 9 electioneering communication against the FEC's Schedule E dump, bulk files and raw filings on the X6b audit: 1,230 24/48-hour notices whose periodic report is also held, 659 memo lines and 31 duplicate pulls count once or not at all; audits X36 and X52 of 2026-10-10 and 2026-10-11 then settled the remaining notice and periodic pairs and count only each expenditure's latest non-memo line, and audit X57 of 2026-10-12 restored 80 latest non-memo lines held as context and set 4 counted copies to context) is the full spending of committees such as SMP, House Majority PAC and LCV Victory Fund, of which only a small, itemised share came from the network; committees with no itemised network receipt in the record (Women Vote, Protect Our Future, Senate Leadership Fund and 212 others) are held as context and not counted. Type 1 counts only lines whose contributor is an individual; type 4b only items whose contributor is the lobbyist, dated while the filing's registrant was registered for a network client (PLAN §1.3; windows from the LDA record, audit X33a: 3,281 items, $3.7M, to sitting members fall outside every window, most before the firm took any network client, and are context; both counts are drawn (Kevin, 2026-09-29): A, the firm registered for a network client, 293 members and $1.2M; B, the lobbyist also named for that client on its registration or quarterly reports, 268 members, 842 items and $1.0M). OpenFEC rate limits left Schedule A pulls unverifiable for several members; those rows are excluded, so counts are floors.

## 02. Public First family money in and out, by race

[`congress-02-public-first-family-by-race.jpg`](figures/congress-02-public-first-family-by-race.jpg) · [`.png`](figures/congress-02-public-first-family-by-race.png) · [`.html`](figures/congress-02-public-first-family-by-race.html)

**What it shows.** The Public First family (Public First, Public First Action, Jobs and Democracy PAC, Defending Our Values PAC) received $40.0M from Anthropic PBC (two gifts to the 501(c)(4), known from Anthropic's own statements because the c4 itemises no donors), $1.0M from Dario Amodei and $3.3M from other individual contributors (not named; private individuals) on FEC Schedule A, plus $28.7M of transfers within the family; its largest aggregate support of one candidate was $15.0M for Alexander Bores (NY) across 115 expenditure lines (the largest single line $949K), followed by seven-figure support for Foushee (D) and De La Cruz (R). Public First Action is a 501(c)(4) whose unitemised donors stay unattributed; Anthropic's $40M rests on issuer statements, not filings. Amounts are as filed; later amendments appear only where a superseding row exists. Three FEC line-15 entries from a vendor (Bryson Gillette, LLC, $1.04M to Jobs and Democracy PAC) are refunds of operating expenditures, not contributions, and are held as context (audit X33).

## 04. Lobbying spend and the lobbyists' Hill origins

[`congress-04-lobbying-spend-and-hill-origins.jpg`](figures/congress-04-lobbying-spend-and-hill-origins.jpg) · [`.png`](figures/congress-04-lobbying-spend-and-hill-origins.png) · [`.html`](figures/congress-04-lobbying-spend-and-hill-origins.html)

**What it shows.** Anthropic's disclosed lobbying spend rose from $280K paid to outside firms in 2023 to $720K (2024), $3.1M (2025, the amended Q2 filing counted once) and $3.5M in the first two quarters of 2026 on its own reports, which include what it paid its outside firms (the firms themselves reported $320K, $680K and $1.5M); Americans for Responsible Innovation spent $1.5M in 2025; the Center for AI Safety Action Fund's 2024 spend is $270K and OPAF's 2026 spend $220K once a termination report and a duplicate submission count once; RAND's Mercury filings for 2025 Q4 and 2026 Q1 were each amended to 'no activity, income under $5,000' on 2026-04-24, so RAND's 2025 spend in the record is the $8K of its amended Q3 and 2026 shows nothing (lane C134 read the print pages). Anthropic's 2016–2026 LD-1 and LD-2 filings disclose a prior covered government position for a lobbyist in 56 distinct filings (one filing UUID counted once; all-placeholder fields are not disclosures), ahead of OPAF (25), ARI (21), IFP Action (9, counting the filings made for it through Slevin Advisory), FLI (7), CAIS (6), Encode AI (5), FAS (4) and the Foundation for American Innovation (2); Anthropic's line joins its in-house reports and those of its outside firms (Aquia Group, Tower 19, Continental Strategy, Ballard Partners and others); Fathom's four filings are context, since Fathom is a context-tier client (PLAN §1.1a, X13b; audit X33); look-alike clients returned by a METR substring (Metropolitan Life, Metropolitan Water and transit districts) are context and not drawn.

**What cuts against it.** LD-2 amounts are rounded; an in-house filer's expenses already include its payments to outside firms, so the two are not added. A covered-position disclosure is a job history, not evidence the position was used. Filings before 2016 (RAND's and FAS's among them) are held and excluded from both panels.

## 05. The revolving door, both directions

[`congress-05-revolving-door-both-directions.jpg`](figures/congress-05-revolving-door-both-directions.jpg) · [`.png`](figures/congress-05-revolving-door-both-directions.png) · [`.html`](figures/congress-05-revolving-door-both-directions.html)

**What it shows.** 28 people moved from the network to Hill offices, committees, congressional commissions and agencies on a primary document: 26 are Horizon fellows (25 with a placement on the programme's own roster, lane C130, and one from another Horizon page, placed at the Government Accountability Office), and two are members of Anthropic's advisory council who serve on the U.S.-China Economic and Security Review Commission. TechCongress placements of a further 129 fellows are held as context under the roster criteria (Kevin's E6 decision, 2026-10-11). Counting each office or committee once whatever its spelling (majority, minority and party staff and subcommittees folded in; audit X33), ten hosts have two each (the Senate Homeland Security, House Foreign Affairs, House Energy and Commerce and House Science committees, the Heinrich, Rounds, Young and Banks offices, the U.S.-China commission and the National Security Commission on Emerging Biotechnology) and 14 other hosts one each (one record per person once middle initials and spelling variants are folded). Three NSCAI commissioners are context: NSCAI is an executive-branch commission (PL 115-232 §1051), not a Hill office. 64 moved from the Hill to network organisations or to lobbying firms working for network clients, 13 of them to Anthropic and 9 to ARI, with Kountoupes Denham and Aquia Group (Anthropic's registrants) next.

**What cuts against it.** Taking a job is not evidence about the work done in it. A fellowship placement is a stint in a host office arranged through the programme and the fellow: Horizon says it introduces fellows to prospective hosts and lets the fellows and hosts decide, and that it fully funds the fellows, TechCongress provides a stipend and benefits. An executive-branch post (the AI Safety Institute at NIST) is not a Hill role and is held as context. Rows with only one role established are held but not drawn. Staff directories that returned 403/404 leave gaps the reviewers recorded.

## 06. Federal money to the network

[`congress-06-federal-money-to-the-network.jpg`](figures/congress-06-federal-money-to-the-network.jpg) · [`.png`](figures/congress-06-federal-money-to-the-network.png) · [`.html`](figures/congress-06-federal-money-to-the-network.html)

**What it shows.** Federal awards with a positive obligation bound to a network entity (42) total about $65.1M: RAND ($63.1M), CNAS ($1.1M), the Carnegie Endowment ($0.8M), a CSET conference grant ($50K, through Georgetown University) and one small Anthropic award ($19K); Defense ($45.5M) and Homeland Security ($16.6M) are the main awarding agencies. Each organisation counts only for the lines of work the network funds there (AI and AGI strategy; technology and security; biosecurity) and only from the date the network first funded that line (PLAN §1.1a amendments of 2026-09-28; start dates in research/network_line_starts.csv): RAND's AI line from January 2020 and its technology-security and biosecurity lines from 2023, so its earlier federal work in those fields is context. The over-exclusion audits of 2026-09-28 (X26, X26b, X30) also returned held-out awards in those lines, among them a $27.4M study of the strategic implications of artificial general intelligence. One award attributed to RAND belonged to Washington University and one obligation had been recorded at a transaction value; both corrected on the Codex audit. The final pass (audit X33, 2026-09-29) applied the rule where it had been missed: four awards dated before their organisation's first network funding (three FAS, one CNAS), seven CNAS awards whose text names no funded line (X30 had held them), and the FY24 NDAA table lines for RAND's Army and Air Force research centres ($37.1M and $38.4M, outside the network-funded line) are context. So is the one proposed amendment naming RAND (SA 3562 to S. 2296, a study of pharmaceutical-ingredient sourcing, ordered to lie on the table; supervisor decision after audit X33b): no bill text in the record names a network organisation for its network-funded work. A bill that merely cites a RAND report is not a provision and is context. Kept 42 awards: audit X42 (2026-10-11) set copies of held awards and FAS awards outside its funded lines to context and left 19 awards waiting on a project-level line, of which audit X58 (2026-10-12) restored one CNAS contract on AI-enabled bioweapons ($211,588) to evidence, confirmed four outside the funded lines (one, FG1984, pending a project allocation) and left 15 unresolved; in a second round (lane C176, audit X61, 2026-10-12) CNAS's own House witness disclosures (2021, 2023, 2025) named ten of them by contract number with their subjects, and the Air Force contract 'Developing a U.S. National Technology Strategy' ($784,468, 2020) now counts, once; three that X61 would also count are held as context pending a scope decision (the industrial-policy project, $1.78M, is shared with CNAS's economics programme without an allocation; the precision-guided warfare information study, $533K over two contracts, is a Defense Program study of military command), three are outside the funded lines (a defense-budget study and two escalation studies), and eight stay unresolved; X33's held-out counts (56 dated before first funding, 5 outside the funded line plus the two research-centre table lines, and 7 CNAS awards without an identified line) predate X42 and are not recomputed here.

**What cuts against it.** An award to an organisation is not a payment to a person. Obligations differ from outlays. CAISI is a government body, so appropriations to it are provisions, not network money, and are listed without a dollar total. Name-match hits the reviewer could not bind (a biotech firm, a consulting LLC) are held as bounded negatives and not drawn.

## 09. Who spends on members, and how much of that money came from the network

[`congress-09-ie-committees-and-network-receipts.jpg`](figures/congress-09-ie-committees-and-network-receipts.jpg) · [`.png`](figures/congress-09-ie-committees-and-network-receipts.png) · [`.html`](figures/congress-09-ie-committees-and-network-receipts.html)

**What it shows.** House Majority PAC ($12.0M for, $183M against sitting members), SMP ($27.1M / $164M) and LCV Victory Fund ($32.3M / $32.2M) are the three committees with the largest support-plus-opposition spending totals on sitting members (MoveOn names the most members, 103); the network's itemised receipts into them are $5.3M, $26.0M and $7.2M, counted line by line as the A7 classification left them. ## 10. Lobbyists' own money, by firm, cycle and recipient

[`congress-10-lobbyists-own-contributions.jpg`](figures/congress-10-lobbyists-own-contributions.jpg) · [`.png`](figures/congress-10-lobbyists-own-contributions.png) · [`.html`](figures/congress-10-lobbyists-own-contributions.html)

**What it shows.** Lobbyists gave $1.2M of their own money to sitting members while their firm was registered for a network client, led by Kountoupes Denham ($344K), Fierce Government Relations ($166K), Tarplin, Downs & Young ($147K) and Navigators Global ($129K). By cycle: $171K (2022), $339K (2024) and $710K (2026 so far), split between the parties. Top recipients are Katherine Clark, Ronny Jackson, Steve Scalise, Frank Pallone and Mike Johnson, each $33K or less. Until 2026-09-29 the figure counted every item these firms' lobbyists reported, $5.3M; audit X33a dated each firm's registrations for network clients from the LDA record, and 3,281 items ($3.7M) given before the firm took a network client, after it stopped, or between registrations are held as context (its check of the excluded side, all 86 items of $10,000 or more and a seeded sample of 200, found none inside a window). The figure gives two counts (Kevin, 2026-09-29): A, above, counts a gift while the lobbyist's firm was registered for a network client; B also requires the lobbyist to be named for that client on its registration or quarterly reports, and comes to $1.0M (842 gifts to 268 members; by firm, Kountoupes Denham $267K, Fierce $166K, Tarplin $147K, Navigators $69K; by cycle $149K, $265K and $612K). Which count fits depends on the question: A measures the firms the network hired; B narrows to the lobbyists the client's own filings name. B is a filing-based period, not proof of who worked when: a registration lists lobbyists from its effective date, a quarterly report names who lobbied that quarter, and B runs to 90 days after the last quarter naming the person, a cut-off the lobbying rules do not set (audit X35). Audit X35 also set six gifts on Ridge Path Strategies' organisation-filed report to context: they are the firm's own contributions, not a lobbyist's. Audit X33 also set 366 duplicate items (a second copy of one printed item, or an original an amendment restates) to context, and audit X34 eleven more copies of items the filing prints once (older hard-money copies of LD-203 items and a third copy of a twice-printed line). One Continental Strategy LD-203 prints a $3,500 item to Carlos Gimenez twice; the record holds both lines (the second collected by lane C164 and checked by audit X43, which found no item identifier to show whether they are two gifts or one printed twice).

**What cuts against it.** Hop 2: the client did not direct the money and these firms serve many other clients. A contribution is not a vote. LD-203 items whose contributor is a PAC or another person rather than the lobbyist are held as context (165 found by X1b, 181 more by the C138 census of every item); an 80-item document sample on the X4a audit bound ten blank-member items to sitting members from the matching FEC receipt. One LD-203 line of $5,000,000 to a leadership PAC (LC5691) is held out as a filer error: the audit lanes found the PAC itemised $0 from that lobbyist in February 2015 and a $250 earmark through ActBlue.

## 15. Travel and events: how the network appears

[`congress-15-travel-and-events.jpg`](figures/congress-15-travel-and-events.jpg) · [`.png`](figures/congress-15-travel-and-events.png) · [`.html`](figures/congress-15-travel-and-events.html)
